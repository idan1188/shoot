When you are ready, and when you are guessing

A raise request starts with notes you can point to. If the lines below are still a feeling, you are guessing. A round number does not make the conversation more prepared.

01

Ready

You can write what changed in the role, your current base, an ask, a floor, who decides, and what you will do if they say not now. The figures are yours. They are not a survey.

02

Guessing

You have a percent you heard, a colleague’s number, or a sentence you have not written down. Opening with that is a guess dressed as a request. Pause and write the missing lines.

03

Not yet

The last review is still open, you cannot name what changed, or you already agreed to wait until a date that has not arrived. Do not book the meeting until those are written down.

How this differs from a counter-offer

Asking for a raise and countering an offer are different conversations. Use the page that matches the paper in front of you. Neither one is advice, and neither one promises an outcome.

01

This page — you are asking

You already work there. Nobody has put a new package in front of you. You are requesting a conversation about pay, with notes you prepared. If they have not named a number yet, you are still on this page.

02

Counter-offer — you are replying

They named a package you are already replying to: a retention offer from an employer you work for, or a number inside a process you are already in. The reply is a counter, with ask, target, and walk-away. That preparation sits on the counter salary offer page.

If the offer is retention or you are already replying inside a process, switch to the counter salary offer guide. If the offer is for a role you do not yet hold, use the first job offer guide. If you are about to ask your current manager and nobody has named a package, stay here and then open the salary brief. If the meeting is a promotion interview about title or level, use the promotion interview guide.

Questions

Short answers. They do not replace a conversation with the employer, or with a qualified professional when the decision needs one.

Is this career, legal, or tax advice?

No. This is an educational preparation guide. It does not tell you what you should be paid, when to ask, or how an employer must respond. Check a decision with the employer and, where it matters, a qualified professional.

What is the average salary raise in Israel?

This page does not publish one. There is no market survey, no percent, and no typical shekel figure here. The three numbers are yours. An average you heard somewhere else is not a floor.

When am I ready to ask, versus guessing?

You are guessing while the checklist still has open lines: what changed, the last pay date, current base, an ask, a floor, who decides, and a plan if they say not now. Ticking every line means the notes exist. It does not mean the request will be approved.

Will these email, WhatsApp, or meeting templates get me a raise?

No. The lines are templates to rewrite. They are not scripts that work, and they are not a promise of an outcome. A manager can agree, defer, or say no.

How is asking for a raise different from a counter-offer?

A raise request is something you open inside a job you already have, before anyone has named a new package. A counter-offer is a reply to a retention offer or to a named package already inside a process. Use the counter salary offer guide for that reply. A first job offer is an offer letter or a verbal package for a role you do not yet hold. Use the first job offer guide. This page does not cover those two.
