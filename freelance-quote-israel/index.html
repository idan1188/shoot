When a quote is ready, and when you are guessing

How to price freelance work starts with a yes or a no. If the boxes below are blank, you are guessing. A freelance quote in Israel does not get more true because the number looks round.

01

Ready

You can name the deliverable, the window, what is out of scope, and a floor you would not go under. The number will come from inputs you choose. It is not a post, a rumor, or what everyone charges.

02

Guessing

You have a feeling, a number you heard, or a rate with no unpaid hours and no buffer. Sending that is a guess dressed as a quote. Pause and write the missing lines.

03

Not yet

Scope is still “we’ll figure it out,” revisions are open-ended, or you have not even noted — at a high level — how osek status changes what the client sees. Do not send a price until those are written down.

The live number sits in the rate calculator. This page does not replace it and does not publish a market rate.

Cost floors, as a checklist

Unpaid time, taxes, utilization, and a buffer are questions you answer before you send a client rate quote. They are a preparation heuristic. This page does not run income tax, national insurance, or VAT, and it does not claim those formulas.

01

Unpaid time

Admin, sales, revisions you did not price, and the gap between projects. If the quote only covers hours you hope to bill, the rest is unpaid. Name those hours before you name a rate.

02

Taxes and osek, at a high level

Registration status changes what a client sees on an invoice. This page does not tell you whether to be osek patur or osek murshe, and it does not compute an official tax. The depth is the osek guide. The calculator’s rough uplift is a toggle you set, not a filing.

03

Utilization

A quiet month still has tools, overhead, and your time. A quote that assumes every working hour is billed will look fine until a month is not full. Utilization is a planning input you choose. It is not a statistic this page supplies.

04

Buffer

Scope grows. A buffer is a choice you name — extra time, a revision cap, or a line for changes — not a percentage this page prescribes. If you cannot say what the buffer covers, you do not have one.

Read osek patur vs murshe for how the calculator treats the two statuses. Then put your own inputs in the rate calculator. Neither page is tax advice.

Good, better, best — a package shape

Three tiers are a way to package scope. They are not market prices, not what clients in Israel pay, and not three random shekel amounts. You are offering three sizes of the same job. The live tool prices whichever size you enter.

01

Good

The smallest complete outcome. One deliverable, a revision cap, and a date. If the client only needs this, the quote should still clear the floor you set. Smaller scope is not a reason to ignore unpaid time.

02

Better

The outcome plus the piece clients usually ask for next — still defined. Write what is included so “better” is not a vague upgrade. You are not labeling it expensive because a market said so.

03

Best

The fuller engagement: more rounds, a longer window, or a retainer shape. It is still a scope, with edges. It is not a premium price copied from someone else’s list. If you cannot name what Best adds, do not offer it.

Price a tier in Pricing OS. The section builds a package from scope you type. It does not look up a going rate.

What to write when you send the rate

These are example message shapes for a freelance quote, and for when a client says the rate is too expensive. They are templates you rewrite in your own words. Replace the brackets. Do not send the brackets, and do not treat the lines as a script that wins the work.

Example · template

Sending the quote

Use this shape when scope, window, and your floor are already written. The number in the brackets is yours, from the calculator or from a floor you set. This page does not fill it in.

Here is the quote for [deliverable], delivered by [date]. Included: [scope]. Not included: [out of scope]. Revisions: [cap]. The fee is [your number]. I can start on [date] if this matches what you need.

Example · template

They say it is too expensive

This shape does not apologize for a number you have not checked, and it does not invent a discount. It offers a smaller scope or a later date. A lower fee without a smaller job is a guess again.

I can hold the fee only if the scope stays as written. If [your number] is above what you want to spend, we can send a smaller version: [reduced scope], by [later date], at a fee I will price from that scope — not from a percentage off.

Example · template

They want the work, without the edges

Unlimited revisions, a brief that says you will know it when you see it, or a rush at the same fee. The template names the edge. It does not agree in the room and reprice later from memory.

I can take this on. The quote covers [scope] and [revision cap]. Anything past that is a change, priced before I start it. If you want it in the first fee, tell me now and I will reprice the scope.

After you rewrite one note, open Pricing OS and check the package against the same scope. The tool does not predict what the client will say.

Checklist, then the live tool

Use this as a preparation order. Stop if a step is blank. The last steps leave this page and open the calculator.

  1. Write the deliverable, the date, what is out of scope, and the revision cap. If any line is still open, you are not ready to send a client rate quote.
  2. List unpaid time you are not billing: admin, sales, revisions outside the cap, and gaps between projects.
  3. Note utilization as a planning choice — what share of your hours you expect to bill — and a buffer you can describe. This page does not supply a correct percentage.
  4. At a high level, note how an invoice should read for your status. If that is unclear, read the osek guide before you treat the fee as final. Do not use this page as tax math.
  5. If you are offering more than one option, write Good, Better, and Best as scopes. Leave the shekel lines blank until the calculator fills them from your inputs.
  6. Draft one note from the example shapes above. It is your draft. It is not a script with a guaranteed yes.

Questions

Short answers. They do not replace a conversation with the client, or with a qualified professional when the decision needs one.

Is this tax, legal, or career advice?

No. This is an educational preparation guide. It does not tell you what to charge, which osek status to choose, or how a client must respond. Check a decision with the client and, where it matters, a qualified professional.

What is the average freelance rate in Israel?

This page does not publish one. There is no market survey, no salary band, and no typical shekel figure here. A number you send comes from scope and inputs you choose, then from the live calculator. An average you heard somewhere else is not a floor.

How do I price freelance work before I send a quote?

Write scope and edges first. Then walk the cost-floor checklist: unpaid time, a high-level note on taxes and osek, utilization, and a buffer you can describe. Only then open the rate calculator and enter your inputs. The checklist is a heuristic. It is not official tax math.

Will a too-expensive template keep the client?

No. The lines on this page are examples and templates for shape. They are not a promise of an outcome. A client can accept, ask for less scope, or walk away.

Should a freelance quote include three prices?

Only if each price is a different scope: good, better, and best as packages, not three discounts on the same job. This page does not set those prices. Pricing OS prices the scope you type.
