When the reply is ready, and when you are guessing

A reply after notice starts with notes you can point to. If the lines below are still a feeling, you are guessing. A round number does not make the resignation less real, and it does not accept the counter.

01

Ready

Notice is already given. You can write the notice status, the counter package in your own words, who asked you to stay, and what you will do if you decline. The words are yours. They are not a survey.

02

Guessing

You have a percent you heard, a package you have not written down, or a sentence that would withdraw the resignation before you decided. Opening with that is a guess. Pause and write the missing lines.

03

Wrong page

You have not resigned. Questions

Short answers. They do not replace a conversation with the employer, or with a qualified professional when the decision needs one.

Is this career, legal, or tax advice?

No. This is an educational preparation guide. It does not tell you whether to accept the counter, whether to withdraw a resignation, what you should be paid, or how an employer must respond. Check a decision with the employer and, where it matters, a qualified professional.

What is a typical counter after resignation, or the average bump, in Israel?

This page does not publish one. There is no market survey, no suggested percent, and no typical package here. Notice status and the counter package are yours. Ask and floor, if you want numbers, are yours too. An average you heard somewhere else is not a floor.

When is the reply ready, versus guessing?

You are guessing while the checklist still has open lines: notice is already given, the notice status is written, the counter package is written, you know who made the counter, and you have a plan if you decline. Ticking every line means the notes exist. It does not accept the counter, and it does not withdraw the resignation.

Will these email, WhatsApp, or talking-point drafts get the counter improved?

No. The lines are templates to rewrite. They are not scripts that work, and they are not a promise of an outcome. An employer can hold the counter, change it, or let the notice you already gave run.

How is a counter after I resigned different from retention while I am still employed, a raise, a promotion interview, or a first offer?

This page is for a counter that arrived after you already resigned or gave notice. Retention while you are still employed, and have not resigned, belongs on the counter salary offer guide. A raise with no named package and no resignation belongs on the raise guide. A promotion interview about title or level belongs on the promotion guide. A first external offer for a role you do not hold belongs on the first job offer guide.
