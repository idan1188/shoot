Questions

Short answers. They do not replace a conversation with the employer, or with a qualified professional when the decision needs one.

Is this HR or legal advice?

No. This page helps you draft a thank-you, a follow-up, or a short check-in from words you type. It does not tell you whether to send the note, when to send it, or what the employer will do. It does not contact the employer. Check a decision with the employer and, where it matters, a qualified professional. The lines are drafts and preparation only.

Will this page tell me how long to wait before I follow up?

No. This page does not set a wait and it does not supply an interval. A date of last contact appears only if you type it, and a blank stays a bracket you replace. A window appears in a draft only when you type those words. If you leave that box blank, the draft leaves the wait out.

What do I type?

Type what you already know. Role, company label, who receives a note, date of last contact, a last-contact note, a window you choose, and another note are optional. Blank role, company, name, and date stay brackets. A blank last-contact note, a blank window, and a blank extra note stay out of the draft. This page does not fill them in.

Does ticking the checklist send the note?

No. The ticks are prep notes about what you already know. They do not send the thank-you, the follow-up, or the short check-in. Nothing you type or tick is sent.

Will these note shapes get a reply?

No. The lines are templates to rewrite. They are not scripts that work, and they are not a promise of a reply or of an offer. A bracket is a detail you have not typed. Replace it yourself. Copying a draft only puts the text on your clipboard. This page does not send it, and it does not include a payment phone number. An employer can reply, stay silent, or close the process.

Where do I go if this is the wrong page?

A written offer in hand is the first job offer guide, and the benefit lines on that letter are the offer benefits guide. Answering a named number, or a retention package, is the counter salary offer guide. The expectations question before a written offer is the salary expectations guide. A promotion conversation in a role you already hold is the promotion interview guide. The salary brief and the command center are the live desks.
