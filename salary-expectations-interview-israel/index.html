When you are ready, and when you are guessing

The question is short. The answer is a figure you can point to. If the lines below are still a feeling, or a number you heard about someone else, you are guessing.

01

Ready

You can write the figure you would say, and the floor under it, in digits you chose. You know this is a screen or an interview, not a written offer. You know what you will do if they push. The numbers are yours. They are not a survey.

02

Guessing

You have a percent you heard, a range from a post, or a sentence you have not written down. Opening with that is a guess dressed as an answer. Pause and write the missing lines. This page will not fill them.

03

Questions

Short answers. They do not replace a conversation with the employer, or with a qualified professional when the decision needs one.

Is this career, legal, or tax advice?

No. This is an educational preparation guide. It does not tell you what to ask for, what you should be paid, whether to name a number, or how an employer must respond. Check a decision with the employer and, where it matters, a qualified professional.

What is a typical salary expectation, or the average, in Israel?

This page does not publish one. There is no market survey, no suggested percent, and no comparison with other offers. The ask, the floor, and the note are yours. An average you heard somewhere else is not a floor.

Does ticking the checklist lock my number or accept an offer?

No. You are guessing while the checklist still has open lines. Ticking every line means the notes exist. It does not lock the ask or the floor, and it does not accept an offer.

Will these talking points, or the email and WhatsApp shapes, get me the number?

No. The lines are templates to rewrite. They are not scripts that work, and they are not a promise of an outcome. A recruiter or a manager can push, defer, or end the screen.

How is this different from a written offer, a raise, a promotion, or gross versus net?

This page is for the expectations question in an interview or early screen, before a written offer. A written offer belongs on the first job offer guide. A raise at the job you have belongs on the raise guide. A promotion conversation about title belongs on the promotion guide. Gross versus net wording belongs on the gross and net guide. The live desk is the salary brief and the command center.
